# Slow-strategy screen for a single ₹5,000 CoinDCX wallet

Screened on 3 October 2026. The target is **₹10,000 profit**, which means a final value of **₹15,000**. This earlier 15-day screen follows the existing paper challenge; the user has since specified a 24-hour deadline, evaluated separately in the [one-day feasibility screen](one-day-feasibility.md). The rules were fixed in the [test plan](../research/strategy_plan.md) before the results were run; the [window-level output](../research/strategy_screen_results.json) and [reproducible code](../research/strategy_screen.py) are committed here.

Each rule controls one hypothetical ₹5,000 spot wallet at a time. It uses BTC/INR, ETH/INR, SOL/INR and XRP/INR only. Seventeen non-overlapping 15-day windows run from 18 January 00:00 UTC through 29 September 23:00 UTC, with an exclusive end boundary of 30 September 2026 00:00 UTC. The primary simulation buys or sells at the next hour's open after a signal from completed hourly candles. It charges the configured regular spot fee, GST, measured half-spread, estimated VDA tax on gains, and exit costs on open positions. Accrued estimated tax is reserved from later purchases. The same rules were also tested with twice the measured half-spread and with execution delayed to the next hour's close, as the new forward-paper bots do. Reported next-hour volume is used for an audit, never to select a winning trade in hindsight.

**No rule made money on average after costs. None reached ₹15,000 at the end or at any hourly mark in any window.** The channel breakout was closest to cash because it almost never traded: it placed six orders across all 17 windows and made money in one. It does not show a repeatable profit edge. Cash beat BTC buy-and-hold in 12 windows; the breakout also beat BTC in 12, so beating BTC here is not evidence of skill. The slower strategies lost less than the fast bots in the [earlier research](research.md), largely by paying trading costs less often.

The historical orders produced no zero-volume or over-10%-of-hour-volume warnings on these four pairs. That is a useful plausibility check, not proof of a fill at the simulated price. The forward paper bots delay execution by one hourly candle, defer orders if the completed hour traded less than ten times the order value, and recheck a deferred signal before retrying. A liquid exit can proceed even when a replacement buy is deferred. They reserve estimated tax and try to exit if paper drawdown reaches 20%. They have **no authenticated CoinDCX client** and cannot place real orders. Each starts only when its new code first runs; their forward results are kept separate from the older bots' rankings. A 20% loss brake cannot ensure a 20% maximum loss after a gap or exchange outage.

These results are still limited by one historical crypto market, a surviving set of four coins, one snapshot of spreads measured on 30 September, a fixed fee/tax estimate, and candles rather than historical order books. The four markets were chosen for liquidity, not past returns, but that does not remove selection bias. The whole period had been partly examined in [earlier repository research](research.md), so this is **not** a clean out-of-sample proof. The only genuinely new evidence will be observations recorded after the paper bots start. CoinDCX's [fee schedule](https://coindcx.com/fees/) and [API documentation](https://docs.coindcx.com/) can change; actual account fees, the user's tax jurisdiction and the current [Income-tax Act, 2025, section 194(1), table row 4](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf) need verification before any live pilot.

The wider search was not confined to chart rules. Time-series trend and crypto cross-sectional momentum are supported as research topics by [Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum) and [Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882), but their findings in other instruments and venues do not pay CoinDCX INR fees for this wallet. Classic [pairs trading](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/19/3/797/1645003) needs a short leg; [market making](https://math.nyu.edu/faculty/avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf) needs order-book depth, queue position and adverse-selection data; and [crypto basis carry](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm) needs actual CoinDCX futures prices, funding and hedge execution. The available candle archive cannot validate any of those as a money-making CoinDCX bot. Previously tested grids, dip-buying, funding signals, AI forecasts and faster trend rules also failed after realistic costs or relied on stale prices and uncertain fills.

Triangular arbitrage received a separate **read-only live-book check**. The [reproducible scanner](../research/arb_snapshot.py) fetched the public INR/USDT and BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP INR and USDT books together at 09:50 UTC on 3 October; their timestamps spanned 3.7 seconds. Its [dated output](../research/orderbook_snapshot.json) checks eight INR → coin → USDT → INR routes, including the reverse direction, at the best displayed prices. The best gross quote was **+0.09%** for the reverse BTC route, but its estimated return was **−1.28%** after two regular INR trade fees and an optimistic 0.17% coin-to-coin fee, all plus GST. Every route's after-fee upper bound was negative (−1.28% to −1.95%). Market-order depth and lot rounding cannot improve those best-quote bounds; movement between the three non-atomic legs could help or hurt and adds risk. One snapshot cannot say whether a future opportunity will appear; no arbitrage order path was added. Fee assumptions come from CoinDCX's [spot fee schedule](https://coindcx.com/fees/) and [published coin-to-coin fee tier](https://coindcx.com/vip/), and must be checked against the actual account.

Reproduce locally with `python3 -m research.strategy_screen --history-dir /path/to/local/history`. The source candle archive stays local because it is 206 MB; its SHA-256 digest in the output is `526e123aad42cea03df330c654dc7a02e95f3c6cd2447a03a3f287520845265f` across the four input files. The results JSON contains every window, order count, drawdown, fill warning and cost stress result. A separate calculation from those rows matched its reported means, medians and profitable-window counts.
